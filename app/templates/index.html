Scenario Synthesis: What the Cryptographic Evidence Tells the Bank

🏛️
1. Why DES Survived for Decades in ATM Networks

In physical ATMs transmitting 8-byte PIN blocks, 6-digit trace numbers, and 16-digit card numbers over ISO 8583 / ANSI X9.8 lines, DES's 64-bit block matches the data size with 33%–50% lower byte overhead than AES-CBC and finishes in under 25 microseconds. Because the payload was small, banks experienced zero performance penalties, cementing DES into global ATM hardware.

⚠️
2. Why DES Collapses in Modern Customer Portals

When migrating to modern e-KYC and digital banking, customers upload 500 KB KTP scans, 2 MB e-statements, and 8–50 MB ATM CCTV footage. In software, DES takes over 40× more CPU cycles than hardware-accelerated AES-NI, locking server threads for hundreds of milliseconds. Coupled with DES's 56-bit key exhaustibility and Sweet32 64-bit block collision vulnerability, DES is strictly disqualified for digital customer data.

🌊
3. Why RC4 Breaks Anti-Fraud Video Scrubbing

While RC4 is fast in pure software (~400 MB/s) and adds zero padding bytes, its internal state machine cannot seek arbitrarily. Investigating ATM fraud requires scrubbing to arbitrary video offsets via HTTP 206 Partial Content. RC4 requires discarding gigabytes of keystream ($O(N)$), whereas block ciphers resume instantly ($O(1)$).

🔒
4. Why AES-256 + HMAC-SHA256 is the Bank's Mandate

AES-256 paired with Encrypt-then-MAC (HMAC-SHA256) fulfills UU PDP Art. 35 obligations by guaranteeing confidentiality, $O(1)$ video streaming, and non-negotiable tamper detection that prevents attackers from altering transaction ledgers, dispute amounts, or customer identity records.
